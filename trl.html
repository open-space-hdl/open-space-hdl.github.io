What the levels mean for our IP cores

The levels are those of ISO 16290, adopted by ECSS as ECSS-E-AS-11C. The ECSS handbook ECSS-E-HB-11A explains how to apply them; for components such as IP cores, its Annex B (Table B-1) lists the milestones of every level, and ESA uses this table for IP cores as well. Our IP cores are VHDL soft IP for FPGAs: properties of the device such as the package, the temperature range, total ionising dose (TID) and single-event latch-up (SEL) are covered by the qualification of the target FPGA, and the IP core has to show that it works on that device. This gives the following meaning for each level:

TRLMeaning for an IP coreEvidence
1 Basic functions defined: the standard and the scope of the implementation are identified. Specification
2 In development: first modules simulated, first performance estimates. First testbenches
3 The IP core is fully verified by simulation, including its failure modes. Regression, coverage closure, compliance matrix, fault injection in simulation, verification report
4 Synthesized and implemented with timing closure on a representative FPGA and tested on hardware in the laboratory against its functional and performance requirements, ideally with an independent link partner. Synthesis and timing report, hardware test plan and test report
5 Running on the radiation-tolerant target FPGA; a single-event effects (SEE) test, such as a heavy-ion or proton beam test with the IP core in operation, confirms the effectiveness of its mitigation (ECC, TMR, safe state machines) and quantifies the error rates. Operation over the temperature range is demonstrated. SEE test plan and test report
6 Integrated in a representative unit (engineering model) with its real link partners and validated in the intended application environment; released with frozen requirements. Data package: user guide, verification and validation report
7 Part of an FPGA design qualified according to ECSS-E-ST-20-40 and ECSS-Q-ST-60-02 in a qualified unit, including radiation hardness assurance. Qualification of the unit
8 Part of a flight model that has passed acceptance and meets the mission requirements. Flight model acceptance
9 Flight proven: typically two years of nominal operation in orbit. Operation data
